Full-core AIG depth audit, 2026-11-04

Finding: The baseline depth of 3006 is reproducible, but does not by itself
establish a frontend error. A single combinational balancing pass reduces it
to 195 with unchanged input, output, and latch counts. The concrete evidence
supports substantial unbalanced structure in the current AIG representation.
No additional functional frontend defect was identified in this audit.

Probe used only the retained final baseline AIG, with no RTL lowering,
correspondence run, mapped-netlist generation, or modification of existing
artifacts:
  abc -c 'read_aiger .../base_core_final/core_yosys.aig;
          strash; print_stats; balance -l; print_stats'
Full command and output: depth_balance_probe.log.

                           inputs  outputs  latches     ANDs    levels
Read/strash                 167415      141   167333   2689341      3006
After balance -l            167415      141   167333   2677278       195

An independent topological pass over the unprocessed binary AIG confirms
that its largest raw chains terminate at latch-next-state inputs, not the
ordinary Core outputs. Its raw longest chain is 5894 AND nodes; the largest
raw ordinary-output depth is 20. These raw numbers count every serialized
gate without ABC's simplification and therefore are not the same metric as
the post-read/strash level count. The deepest recorded next-state endpoints
begin at latch index 150672. Detailed structural results are preserved in
depth_raw_aig_audit.json; they carry no source-name attribution.

Limits and interpretation:
- These are two-input-AND graph levels, not mapped-cell delay, maximum clock
  frequency, or a timing result. Inverters are represented on AIG edges.
- The final AIG lacks an internal-node name map. The retained normalized
  Verilog has hierarchical aliases, but this audit did not rerun its large
  elaboration solely to recover a path map. It would be speculation to
  attribute the deepest chain specifically to cache muxes, arithmetic,
  scheduler logic, or a frontend bug without that mapping.
- The reviewed integer and FP dividers are iterative RTL. Their presence
  does not justify assuming an unrolled division critical path.
- Balancing explains why the magnitude 3006 is not a stable physical metric;
  it is not a proof that every prior frontend transformation is correct.
  Retain the separate source-patch and proof audits for those claims.
- No change to the experiment or manuscript is required solely because
  ABC initially reports level 3006. Do not promote that number to a timing
  claim or use it alone as evidence of invalid synthesis.
